聚形成字 Juxing

Juxing (“gathering shapes into characters”) builds the Han characters of Terrarum Sans Bitmap: all 27,584 CJK Unified Ideographs and Extension A, as 15×15 pixel glyphs. Nobody draws 27,584 glyphs. Instead, people draw the pieces characters are made of, glyphlettes, and Juxing assembles every character from them.

Draw 氵 once and it serves over 1,400 characters; draw 亻, 扌, 木 and a couple of thousand more and the everyday characters are complete. The workbench you are in shows what to draw next, lets you draw it, and shows you the result in real text straight away.

How it works

  1. Decompose. Every character is split into its parts using open character data (清 = 氵 + 青, 青 = 龶 + 月 …).
  2. Lay out. Each part gets a box inside the 15×15 body. How big a part is and where it sits is measured from a well-made reference font, Chiron Hei HK.
  3. Plan. Juxing works out which pieces are worth drawing, and in which order: the pieces that complete the most common characters come first.
  4. Draw. You draw each piece once, at its largest size, on a drawing sheet.
  5. Resize. The other sizes of a piece are derived from your drawing by a stroke-preserving resizer. Where it can't do a size well, it asks for a drawing of that size too.
  6. Assemble. Characters are put together from the pieces and then fine-tuned for even spacing and balance, after Lai, Yeung & Pong's glyph-composition method (1996).

Simple shapes such as 口 日 田 工 土 are drawn by code and need no drawing at all, though a hand drawing of the same piece always wins.

Words you'll meet

glyphlette
A piece drawn by hand: a radical, a part, a larger chunk, or a whole character.
slot
A piece in a box of a given size, written 木@5x15: 木, 5 pixels wide and 15 tall.
frame
The outside of an enclosing piece (囗 冂 广 辶 …), drawn over the whole box with its inside kept empty: 辶@15x15/⿺.
family
The sizes one drawing serves: the same piece in tall, square or wide boxes. 木 on the left of 林 and 木 under 呆 are drawn differently, so they are different families.
base
The one drawing of a family, at its largest size.
derived
Any other size of a family, made from the base by the resizer.
declined
A size the resizer can't make well. It needs a drawing of its own.
override
A hand drawing of one exact size, replacing its derived version.
draft
An override you have opened but not saved yet. It starts from the resizer's version and only takes a cell on the sheets when you first save it.
generated
A simple piece drawn by code (口 日 目 田 …).
exemplar
The common character a glyphlette is shown in while you draw it.

Drawing, step by step

  1. Issue. In the Worklist, issue the next glyphlettes. They get cells on a drawing sheet, most useful first. (Cells never move. A cell the plan no longer uses is an orphan: its editor offers to retire it, which frees the cell for the next glyphlette issued.)
  2. Draw. The Editor opens the cell with guides: the blue box is where the piece goes, the red area inside a frame stays empty, and the rest of the exemplar is shown around it, grey where its parts aren't drawn yet and faint white where they are, so you can line things up. The live preview shows every size derived from your drawing and the characters that use it, as you draw. Stuck for a start? T shows a suggestion traced from Chiron Hei HK.
  3. Save (Ctrl+S). The drawing goes into its sheet and is used everywhere at once.
  4. Check the sizes. Under “Sizes of this family”, click any size you don't like to override it: it opens as a draft, starting from the resizer's version. The Families tab lists every family's sizes, the worst resized ones first.
  5. Look at real text in the Text tab, and click a character to see what it is made of.

The sheets are ordinary 32-bit TGA files, so Krita or any editor that keeps the alpha channel works too, just not on the same sheet at the same moment as the web editor.

How to draw

  • Draw from scratch, or from a suggestion. Never trace or copy pixels from another font, WenQuanYi above all. The one exception is the editor's Suggest (T): a glyphlette traced from Chiron Hei HK, shown as orange dots for you to draw over. Chiron Hei HK is under the same licence as this font (SIL OFL 1.1), which allows this as long as it is credited.
  • Strokes are 1 pixel wide, in opaque white. Everything else on a sheet is a guide and is redrawn by Juxing.
  • Draw each family at its largest size, the size the worklist gives you. Smaller sizes are made from it.
  • The box is the space available, not a size to fill. A piece may use less. Ink outside the box is kept, for strokes that reach across to join a neighbour (the hook of 小 meeting 幺).
  • Serifs are one pixel at most: the legs of 口 below its bottom stroke, for instance.

What the resizer keeps

A stroke stays a stroke: a diagonal step stays diagonal. When a dot is made narrower, its runs get shorter, never its steps.

A piece made of separate strokes, like 氵, is resized stroke by stroke, so each dot keeps its shape. Where a size still comes out wrong, override it: your drawing always wins.

Sources and licences

Juxing uses open data only:

  • Unihan and related Unicode data (Unicode License v3): stroke counts, variants, commonness.
  • BabelStone IDS (Andrew West; rights waived): how characters decompose.
  • CNS 11643 stroke data (Ministry of Digital Affairs, Taiwan; Open Government Data Licence v1.0): stroke types.
  • Chiron Hei HK (The Chiron Hei HK Project Authors; SIL Open Font License 1.1): the visual target for sizes and positions, which are measured from it, and the source of the editor's suggestions, traced from it on request. A glyphlette started from a suggestion carries its licence and credit with it.

GPL data is deliberately not used: neither WenQuanYi, whose glyphs Juxing replaces, nor the CHISE/cjkvi IDS data. The composition method follows P.-K. Lai, D.-Y. Yeung and M.-C. Pong, A Heuristic Search Approach to Chinese Glyph Generation Using Hierarchical Character Composition (1996). Terrarum Sans Bitmap is licensed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1.

Behind the workbench

Everything here is also on the command line, in Juxing/ of the Terrarum Sans Bitmap repository:

make servethis workbench
make planthe drawing order, in out/worklist.tsv
juxing.py show 清how a character is laid out and what it needs
juxing.py derived --scorethe resizer against the sizes drawn by hand
makeassemble the font sheet, out/juxing.tga
